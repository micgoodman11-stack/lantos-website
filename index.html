LM-02 · SERVICES

Services

Design and specialist advice across the life of every airway device.

What we do

Specialist advice for tracheostomy and laryngectomy care.

Airway devices are small, but the wrong choice, the wrong size or an unfamiliar product at the bedside can put a patient at risk. We bring focused expertise to every decision about them.

01

Device selection & formulary review

An independent review of the tubes and laryngectomy products you stock, matched to your patients and settings.

  • Current range and usage audit
  • Sizing and variant rationalisation
  • Formulary recommendations
02

Product evaluation

Structured comparisons and clinical trials of competing devices, scored by your own clinicians.

  • Evaluation criteria and scoring
  • Ward and clinic trial design
  • Patient and staff feedback
03

Device design & development

We design tracheostomy and laryngectomy devices from first concept to detailed specification, shaped by the clinicians and patients who use them.

  • Concept design and prototyping
  • Clinical user testing
  • Design specifications and documentation
04

Airway safety review

A close look at how devices are used day to day, from bedside equipment to tube changes and emergencies.

  • Bedside emergency equipment checks
  • Tube change and decannulation protocols
  • Incident and near-miss review
05

Education & competency

Training programmes that give nurses, therapists and carers confidence with the specific devices you use.

  • Tube care, cuff and inner cannula management
  • Laryngectomy stoma and HME care
  • Competency frameworks
06

Care pathways & discharge

Joined-up pathways so patients leave hospital with the right equipment and a community team that knows their device.

  • Hospital-to-home transition planning
  • Community care recommendations
  • Voice and swallowing rehabilitation links

Devices we work on

From the first tube to life after laryngectomy.

Our work covers the full range of airway devices used before, during and after tracheostomy and laryngectomy, including the ventilators many of these patients rely on. We bring design and clinical insight to each of them.

LM-03 · PROCESS

Our process

A clear, documented path from the first audit to the annual review.

How we work

Five stages, one accountable team.

Whether you are reviewing a single ward's tracheostomy tubes or standardising laryngectomy care across an NHS trust, every engagement follows the same path. Each stage ends with a document you keep.

  1. 01

    Assess

    We audit the devices you use, how they are used and any incidents involving them.

    Baseline audit
  2. 02

    Review

    We compare your range against current evidence, guidelines and available products.

    Options report
  3. 03

    Select

    Design specifications, prototypes and clinical trials to support your decision.

    Recommendation
  4. 04

    Implement

    Staff education, bedside protocols and a planned switchover to new devices.

    Rollout plan
  5. 05

    Sustain

    Follow-up audits, incident review and updates as products and guidance change.

    Annual review

What you receive

A document at every stage.

Every recommendation is written down and traceable, so your clinical and governance teams can follow the reasoning long after the engagement ends.

DOC 01Baseline audit

A record of the devices in use, how they are used and any related incidents, with priorities for action.

DOC 02Options report

Your current range set against guidelines, evidence and the alternatives available, with the trade-offs explained.

DOC 03Recommendation

A documented recommendation, with design specifications and trial results where relevant.

DOC 04Rollout plan

Training, bedside protocols and a timetable for introducing changes safely across wards and community teams.

DOC 05Annual review

A yearly check of outcomes, incidents and new guidance, with any updates you need to make.

LM-04 · ABOUT

About us

A specialist consultancy, founded in 2008 and based in Oxfordshire.

Who we are

One specialty, studied closely.

Founded in 2008 by biomedical engineer Vilmos Lantos, Lantos Medical is a design and consulting firm dedicated to tracheostomy and laryngectomy devices. These patients depend on their device to breathe, and for many, to speak. We believe the decisions behind those devices deserve specialist attention.

We work alongside your ENT surgeons, intensivists, nurses, speech and language therapists and respiratory physiotherapists. Every device we design or recommend is shaped by the people who rely on it every day.

Our field

Two airways, two kinds of device.

A tracheostomy and a laryngectomy both leave a patient breathing through the neck, but the anatomy is different, and so are the devices, the risks and the care. We work on both.

Simplified schematics, not to scale.

How we think

Four principles behind every project.

Design-led

We approach every device as engineers, grounding each design and recommendation in clinical evidence and your patients' needs.

Specialist

We focus on tracheostomy, laryngectomy and the ventilators that support these patients, so we know the product range and its pitfalls in detail.

Patient-centred

Breathing safety comes first, followed closely by voice, swallowing, comfort and independence at home.

Documented

Every recommendation is recorded and traceable, so your clinical and governance teams can follow the reasoning.

LM-05 · TEAM

Our team

Engineering-led advice from the founder of Lantos Medical.

Founder

Vilmos Lantos

Founder & Principal Consultant

Vilmos Lantos founded Lantos Medical in 2008 to design, and give engineering-led advice on, the devices patients depend on. Under his leadership the firm has focused on tracheostomy and laryngectomy, where small differences in device design, sizing and use matter most.

Vilmos holds a Master of Engineering (MEng) in Biomedical Engineering from Imperial College London. His training in how medical devices are designed, tested and regulated shapes the firm's approach: every recommendation starts from how a device performs for the patient, and is set out clearly enough for clinicians, engineers and governance boards to follow.

He works closely with ENT surgeons, intensive care teams, nurses, speech and language therapists and respiratory physiotherapists, translating between the technical detail of a device and what it means at the bedside and at home.
